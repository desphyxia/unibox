# Avatar World: Findings (Videos, Reviews, Other Sources)

Status as of 2026-10-12. Same assessment as for Toca Boca World (`docs/toca-boca-world-findings.md` and `docs/toca-boca-world-quantified.md`), done for Avatar World by Pazu Games, in one document. Raw coding is in `docs/data/avatar-*.csv`. Text only: no videos, screenshots or review text are stored in the repo. Key findings and recommendations across both games are in `docs/reference-games-comparison.md`.

**How this document is organised.** Evidence comes in three kinds and they are kept apart:

- **A. Video evidence** (41 videos). Sections 3-5.
- **B. Reviews** (3 independent or semi-independent pages, a store-analytics page, 500 recent Google Play reviews). Section 6.
- **C. Other sources** (store listings, the developer's own pages, trade press). Section 7.

Section 8 lists where they agree and conflict. Section 9 turns the numbers into targets for the unicorn game. Section 10 lists what is unknown.

## 2. Method and limits

**Video selection (41).** Chosen from 20 YouTube searches ("first time", "update", "shop", "Avatar Pass", "multiplayer", "kid playing" and so on) before any was watched. Rules: Avatar World only; not "free coins hack/code" scams; spread across 2023-2026 and across the groups in 3.2; unedited footage preferred for time coding. Of 358 Avatar World videos in the search results, 38% are about secrets, free coins or codes, 26% about updates, 17% about stories or routines, 12% about houses and decor, 12% about first impressions or comparisons, 8% about shopping or passes, 5% about multiplayer (a video can count in more than one group). One planned video (A38) downloaded as a 3-minute stub and was replaced; one video was added (A41, the Avatar ID explainer). Combined length of the 41 is 9.3 hours.

**Frame sampling.** Each video was tiled into timestamped contact sheets at a fixed interval (5-155 s depending on length). A local Whisper "base" transcription was made for each video (about 14 of the 42 transcripts have little or no usable speech, and three are in Hindi, Indonesian or Spanish; I read those with that in mind).

**Time coding.** For 21 sessions I labelled every sampled frame with one activity: play in a location, build/decorate, character creator or roster, map, shop/pass/gift screen, loading/title, other (store page, settings, intro or outro). A share is "frames of that type / frames coded". One edited video (A29) is coded but kept out of the pooled shares.

**Reliability check.** I re-labelled 36 randomly chosen frames blind (shuffled, no timestamps, aligned with the same extraction as the sheets) against my labels: **40/40 agreement, kappa 1.0** on the final labels. That is a weak test: the same person (me) labelled the same sheets earlier the same day, 22 of the 40 frames were plain play, and the classes were easy. Earlier tries are not counted: they compared against labels written while images were not displayed (see the process note) and gave 81% and 92%, which shows where the real ambiguity is (a house with an open inventory panel versus one without; a loading vignette versus a map fade). Treat shares as good to roughly plus or minus 5 points, not better.

**Process note on image reading.** For a long stretch of this session, image reads returned a "media removed" placeholder instead of the picture. During that time I wrote notes and a first set of frame labels. I **discarded that first labelling** and re-coded all 21 sessions from rendered sheets once images worked again. Statements below come from re-viewed sheets, transcripts, metadata or fetched pages; notes I could not re-verify (specific coin balances in some shop videos, a few pack prices) were dropped.

**Known biases.**
- Creators choose what to film. The sample over-represents shopping, passes, secrets and building and under-represents ordinary play.
- **Real children are rare.** Three videos show a child on camera or are likely a child (A4, A5, A35); none is a long, unassisted session by a child under about 7. Child time shares rest on 36 minutes.
- Many "kid" videos are role-play story channels by older children or adults.
- The game changed a lot from 2022 to 2026 (shop UI, Avatar ID, City Maker, multiplayer). Every figure carries its date.
- I did not play the game myself.

## 4. Content: what is free, what is paid

### 4.1 Free at the start
- **Map and places:** three creators in 2023, 2024 and 2025 say all locations are free, including the newest beach island and "college" campus. A 2024 Spanish video adds that houses are free and decorations are paid. A 2025 video says "five or six free house makers in a huge free furniture store". The iOS listing is "Free".
- **Characters:** 3 saved characters free (stated in an App Store review on screen in A3, in a Spanish comparison, and in the JustUseApp summary). A comparison video says a paid pack raises avatar slots to 30. **Pets:** 2 free, more by buying a pack (comparison video; a shopper's checklist in A36 includes a "30 pet slots" item).
- **Gifts:** a limited-time free item per week and free items in some updates (creator claims). A Play review on 2026-10-11 says "every update gift" is gone and there are no free packs now.
- **Free coins:** hidden "secrets" in locations pay coins (see 5.2); a "Kitty Bank" on the shop screen holds free coins to claim (A29: claim 150).

### 4.2 Total content
No official counts. What I have:
- Developer page: 6 maps (but 7 world cards listed), 1,170+ avatars.
- A title count of at least 30 distinct places with secret hunts, from Mochapi, Khaohorm Play and other series (lower bound; some are quests, not places).
- The pass screen for January 2025 lists a closet, a bathroom, tiles, a sci-fi movie set, a mermaid movie set, a toddler room and more: **about 7 new packs a month**.
- Pack labels in the shop: **House Maker, Avatar Maker, Pet Maker, Narrative** (story sets with characters and props).

### 4.3 Price points
**iOS in-app purchases (US, 2026-03-25 and the store page 2026-10-12):** Avatar Pass $9.99; AW Coin Pack 200 $1.99; 600 $4.99; 1,250 $9.99; AW starter pack 1,500 $4.99; Avatars Pack $2.99; My First Family $0.99; Babies Pack $2.99; Posh Pups Pack $2.99; **Pazu PLUS monthly $9.99** (added 2026-03-25; a $14.99 coin pack was removed). Google Play: "$0.99-$59.99 per item".

**Coin arithmetic (mine):** $1.99 for 200 (1.0 cent per coin); $4.99 for 600 (0.83); $9.99 for 1,250 (0.80); the $4.99 starter pack is 0.33 cents per coin. **Shop prices seen in frames:** 100 coins (a small avatar pack, "Gingham Squad"), 300 (a dining-room house pack, a baby-shower pack, "Fancy Cats"), 400 (a content-creator room), 700 (an avatar pack), 720 (a 20%-off bundle), 1,960 (a 30%-off spring bundle, was 2,800). So a typical pack is **$2.50-$3.50** and a big bundle **$15-$22**. A Spanish creator (2024) says Toca packs are much cheaper than Avatar World's; I did not verify that.

**The pass:** the Avatar Pass is **$9.99 a month**, cancel any time. It gives every new pack of the month and **3 popular packs you do not own**, and packs claimed during the subscription stay (A15, A16, A18). The screens show "included", "owned", "coming this month" and "new awesome content coming in February".

**Parental gate:** a number pad "Grown-ups only. Please enter your year of birth. We do not store this data." (A20), the same pattern as Toca.

**Cost to own everything:** unknown. The Pass is about **$120 a year**; owning all packs by buying them is not shown anywhere.

### 4.4 Character slots and pets
3 saved characters free; more slots are sold. In the 500-review Play sample only 2 reviews mention the character cap (0.4%), against 6 for Toca (1.2%). A5's creator says "I had to start all over again", which shows a lost-character moment (cause not shown).

### 5.1 Time breakdown (21 sessions, `docs/data/avatar-time-coding.csv`)

What to take from it:
- **Two styles again.** Role-play and story videos spend 65-100% in locations (A11: 100% over 62 minutes, about 16 places, one new place every 4 minutes). Building videos (A10, A22, A26, A28) spend 50-100% in the decorating panel. The same split as in the Toca sample.
- **The creator is the first thing children do.** In all three child sessions the creator opens within the first 2 minutes and takes 12-24% of the video.
- **Loading is visible.** In the child sessions 16% of time is the title screen and loading vignettes (an orange cat-and-alarm-clock animation about every 3-4 minutes in A35: 24%). In adult-narrated sessions it is 3-8%.
- **Shop screens are 4% pooled** but dominate shop videos (46% of A19, 21% of A21). The shop is a browsing catalog with a coin balance and a "restore purchases" link in the corner of every screen.
- **Child sessions barely build.** 4% build in 36 minutes of child play (all of it one gift-unpacking stretch in A5) against 38% in the silent videos. The same direction as the Toca result (child 3% build vs 58% in other sessions).

### 5.2 Variety of things to do
I grouped what players do into **20 activity families**: cooking and serving (pizza job, restaurant, supermarket), hygiene (shower, hair wash), rest, shopping (cashier, ATM, vending machine), music and stage (DJ booth, band, piano), sport (basketball with a scoreboard, beach volleyball, air hockey), pet care (a cat with a poop pile and a "?" bubble), baby and doctor care, gardening and farming (a dig-for-seed task, a fruit orchard), swimming and water, dress-up, hair and make-up salons, school (classroom, lockers, kindergarten), crafts and chores (mopping with a "caution" sign, laundry), travel (train, airport, bus, hotel, family minivan), photography and filming (cameras and ring lights as objects), **hidden-object secrets**, home building, character creation, and gift unboxing. I did not build a per-video presence matrix, so I can say "at least these 20 exist" but not how often each appears.

**What Avatar World has that Toca does not (in these videos):**
- **Secrets with counters.** Each location has a hunt with a visible counter ("Capturing Stars 4/19 found", "Hot Penguins 10/19 found" in one hotel video). Finding things unlocks gift boxes, items and **coins**. 38% of Avatar World YouTube titles in my search are about secrets, free coins or codes: the secrets are the game's community content.
- **A phone with apps** (notebook, grid, magnifier for clues, car). Clues come from detective-hat characters.
- **Mini-games with scoreboards** (a basketball court with a "vs" scoreboard in A31, and the narrator of A3 says they play basketball; a beach volleyball net in A35; an air-hockey table in an arcade in A4).
- **Pets with small reactions.** In A1 a cat leaves a visible poop pile on the floor with a "?" emoji bubble, and the creator remarks that it "needs to poop"; a similar pile appears beside a cat in A39. I did not see what the player can do about it or any consequence, so I cannot call it a need system; nothing else I saw is a need, mood or failure state.
- **Manual weather and time of day.** Rain, snow on the map, a sun/weather button on the map. A 2025 creator says the sun "has recently been changed and become more realistic" and that sunset is possible. It is player-set, not real-time.
- **Hotel, College, K-pop and other seasonal "seasons"** (Season 5 College Life, Season 6 Stardust Grand Hotel).

### 5.3 Onboarding (first-run videos A1-A5, A35)
- **Creator first.** In A2, A3, A4, A5 and A35 the player makes a character before anything else (2-5 minutes, with body types from baby to adult; A1 starts in a house and makes one later).
- **Play within seconds.** Without the creator, A1 is in a house with a cat and a pizza counter inside 30 seconds.
- **Train and map.** A train ride or map choice comes next in A2, A3 and A4.
- **No purchase prompt** is visible in any first-run video; a store page shows "No ads" and "Kid friendly" badges (A3).

### 5.4 Update and free-drop cadence
- **Android versions:** 1.216 (2026-07-09), 1.217 (07-16), 1.218 (07-31), 1.219 (08-08), 1.220 (08-15), 1.221 (08-21), 1.222 (08-28), 1.223 (09-12), 1.224 (09-19), 1.225 (10-01): **10 releases in 84 days**.
- **iOS:** 2.164 on 2026-10-01 with a Hotel Restaurant ("Dinner is served").
- **YouTube update coverage:** one channel posts "all secrets" videos for each new place almost weekly from 2026-04 to 2026-10. The Stardust Grand Hotel arrived in pieces: the hotel (2026-09), a hotel restaurant and event venue (2026-10).
- **Pack cadence:** a monthly pass lineup with a "coming this month" shelf.
- **Free gifts:** older updates carried free gifts; a 2026-10-11 review says they are gone. **Free items still arrive** (a free K-pop drop; a free hotel).

### 5.5 Multiplayer and save
- **Multiplayer (beta in 2024):** a friends button on the title screen opens Host / Join. A host shows "Hosting 1/2" (2024-09) and **"Hosting 1/4"** (2025-05) with a **6-character World ID**; guests type the ID on a keyboard. Players appear as small avatars; a "Grown Ups" link sits on the title screen. A creator in 2026 found a public room ("77777") with others in it.
- **Chat:** a chat-bubble button exists and settings include "Chat (English)". The panel I saw lists short preset phrases; I did not see a free-text keyboard in play (the keyboard shown is for the World ID). Unconfirmed.
- **Avatar ID (since 2025-07):** an icon on the title screen. A grown-up enters their **year of birth**, registers (the store label lists an email address) and gets **cloud save**. Saving and loading are **manual**: "load world" **overwrites** what is on the device, and new changes are not saved until you press save (A41). Several reviews mention failed log-ins and a request to remove the email step.

## 7. Evidence C: other sources (assessed separately)
- **Apple listing (2026-10-12):** free, in-app purchases, English only, 1.57 GB, iOS 15.6+, age rating **4+ and a "Made for Ages 6-8" label** (Toca has the same), released 2022-12-09, v2.164 on 2026-10-01, genres Games / Entertainment / Simulation / Role Playing. **Privacy label:** no "Data used to track you". "Data linked to you": purchase history, **email address**, customer-support content, user ID and device ID, product interaction and crash data, used for app functionality, analytics, personalization **and the developer's advertising or marketing**. (Toca: coarse location plus identifiers, also marketing.)
- **Google Play:** 266 million installs by a scraper estimate (the store shows "100M+"), content rating Everyone, contains ads: no, released 2022-12-12, v1.225.
- **Pazu's claims:** 42 million monthly players; 280 million downloads on its profile (300 million in a 2026 interview, 220 million in 2025); growth "organic, not paid"; a licensing push (Brazil in 2026). Pazu also sells **Pazu+**, a subscription across 50+ older games "with no ads and no in-app purchases"; the trade press says Avatar World sits outside it, while the store lists Pazu PLUS among its in-app items.
- **Market estimates:** AppMagic via Business of Apps (in `docs/concept-state.md`): Avatar World 2025 revenue about **$17M** (iOS + Google Play) against **$88M** for Toca Boca World: roughly a fifth, with more ratings. Low confidence; I found no second source.
- **Developer privacy policy:** effective 2019-04-17, says it does not knowingly collect data from under-18s, says nothing about COPPA, chat or multiplayer, and names no analytics providers.

## 9. What this means for the unicorn game (inferences, not findings)

These are targets to test. The cross-game version, with Toca, is `docs/reference-games-comparison.md`.

1. **A fully free world is the more popular model.** Avatar World has the better rating (4.73 vs 4.29), 86% five-star (72%), half the price complaints and more iOS ratings (1.23M vs 0.90M) in about half the time. Monetising packs and a pass, not places, did not cost it goodwill in these samples (but revenue is about a fifth of Toca's by one estimate).
2. **Give children something to find.** Secrets with counters are the game's biggest community content and its only goal layer. For a unicorn sanctuary, discoverable creatures, tracks and hidden places per island fit the "no failure" rule.
3. **Cloud save must be automatic and safe.** Avatar World's manual save and "load overwrites the device" invites the lost-progress reviews the game already gets. A child should never press "load".
4. **Do not copy the account step.** An email-linked ID with a year-of-birth gate is the opposite of a Kids Category design.
5. **Keep the first 10 minutes short on loading.** In child sessions 16% of time was loading and title screens.
6. **Small pet reactions get noticed.** A creator comments on the cat's poop pile and "?" bubble; a visible, harmless pet need is a cheap hook worth testing.

## 10. What is still unknown
- How much of the game a **young child** plays alone, what they buy (if anything) and what they stumble on.
- **Revenue, conversion and retention.**
- Whether **free-text chat** exists and how multiplayer is moderated.
- Whether a **time limit** exists today.
- The **number of packs and items**, and the cost to own everything.
- Whether **Pazu PLUS includes Avatar World** and whether ads show in the free game today.
- The **hospital, courthouse and other places** I did not see.
