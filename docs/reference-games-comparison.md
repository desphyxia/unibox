# Reference Games: Key Findings and Recommendations (Toca Boca World and Avatar World)

Status as of 2026-10-12. This is the common summary. The evidence is in two separate documents, one per game, and nothing from them is merged here except as a numbered finding:

- Toca Boca World: `docs/toca-boca-world-findings.md` (15 videos, qualitative) and `docs/toca-boca-world-quantified.md` (40 videos, 14 review pages, 500 App Store reviews, other sources).
- Avatar World: `docs/avatar-world-findings.md` (41 videos, review pages, 500 Google Play reviews, other sources).

Recommendations are **inferences**, not findings. Where a number comes from one game only, it says which. The unicorn game's own concept is in `docs/concept-state.md`.

## 2. Key findings (what both games show)

1. **A very large audience is reachable with no needs system.** Both games are open-ended dress-up, house and role-play toys with no goals. That is the evidence that the genre sells without failure states; it is not evidence that needs and moods would sell.
2. **A free world got the better rating.** Avatar World gives away every place and charges for decoration packs and a pass. It has the higher rating, a larger share of five-star ratings and half the price complaints. Toca, with about 10% free, has price as its biggest review theme. Revenue is lower for Avatar World by one estimate, so "free is better" holds for goodwill, not proven for money.
3. **Both cap saved characters at 3 for free.** In both, the cap is a minor but recurring annoyance (about 1% of reviews). Neither blocks play.
4. **Children play in locations and in the creator, and barely build.** Child sessions: 53-63% in locations, 16-18% in the creator, 3-4% building. Adult and creator videos: 20-44% building. Both samples are small (5 and 3 sessions), so read the contrast, not the exact numbers.
5. **The creator is the first thing a child does.** In every first-run video it opens in the first two minutes and takes 12-24% of the first session.
6. **Lost progress and purchases are the most emotional complaint, in both.** Reviews with lost progress have the lowest star averages (about 3.4). Toca's store restore fails for some players; Avatar World's cloud save is manual and loading overwrites the device.
7. **Loading is visible overhead in child sessions.** About 16% of time in Avatar World's child sessions, 22% in Toca's first-run video, against 2-8% in adult sessions.
8. **Both release often and use a free ritual.** Toca has a free Friday gift and a news post about every two weeks. Avatar World ships roughly weekly and has a monthly pack lineup and a pass.
9. **Avatar World adds the only goal layer in the pair.** Per-location hunts with visible "n/19 found" counters, coin rewards and a large community of "secrets" videos (38% of its YouTube titles in my search). Toca has no equivalent.
10. **Both carry marketing identifiers in a 4+ app.** Neither label is something to copy for a Kids Category game.
11. **Neither has breeding, a storybook or creature care beyond small gags.** Your differentiators are untested by this research: nothing here supports or refutes them.

## 3. Recommendations for the unicorn game (inferences)

Numbered so they can be accepted or struck one by one.

1. **Make the first island complete and free**, as already decided. The evidence now supports it from both sides: Toca's mostly paid world draws the most price complaints, and Avatar World's free world is the better-rated game.
2. **Do not cap what a child can keep on the first island**, or make the cap generous. Both games cap saved characters at 3 and it still shows up in reviews; a cap on creatures may also feel like a missing feature given the "complete first island" promise. Sell new islands, new creature families, decor and outfits instead.
3. **Add a no-failure goal layer.** Avatar World's secrets show that children and creators both respond to "find the n of 19". A unicorn equivalent is discoverable creatures, tracks, hidden places and a collection book per island, with no timers or losses. This also gives the parent storybook something to tell.
4. **Make the creature creator the keystone and open it in the first minute.** It is 12-24% of a first session and the most-visited screen for children in both games.
5. **Treat building as a mode for the older child.** Child sessions barely build. That supports Builder mode for ages 8-10 and a "Little" experience (ages 4-7) that is locations and the creator. It also bears on the open decision about Little mode: build Little as exploring, caring and dressing, not as a smaller builder.
6. **Design saves and purchase restore first.** Local autosave with no manual load; purchases restored through the platform account; no email account for the child. Both reference games lose players here, and Avatar World's manual "load world overwrites" is the pattern to avoid.
7. **Keep loading short and hidden.** Budget for 10% or less of a child's first 15 minutes, and use animation rather than a spinner, as both games do.
8. **Hold multiplayer back.** Avatar World's host/join exists but its safety review is thin and its chat is unconfirmed; Toca stays single-player. For ages 4+ in the Kids Category, ship without it and revisit after a compliance review.
9. **Collect no advertising or marketing identifiers**, no email from a child and no location. This is the opposite of both store labels and the clearest compliance difference you can have.
10. **Plan a free weekly ritual and a steady drop cadence.** A free gift each week is the most-praised monetisation feature in Toca's reviews; roughly weekly releases keep Avatar World in the creator-video ecosystem. A small team could start with a free weekly item and a larger drop every two to four weeks.
11. **Parent-gate every purchase and keep it out of play.** Both use a year-of-birth number pad, which is a sound minimum; do not mix free and paid items in one location and do not show locked places as greyed-out map pins (a recent Toca complaint).
12. **Test the differentiators with children, not YouTube.** Visible, harmless needs and a creature bond are the unproven part. A pet's poop pile in Avatar World is noticed by creators, which is a cheap hint, not evidence. Record 4-6-year-olds playing alone on a prototype; neither research round found one.

## 4. What is still unknown

- What a **child aged 4-6 does alone** in either game, how long, what they ask a parent to buy.
- **Revenue, conversion and retention** per game. The only figures are two estimates from one source and company claims.
- Whether **Avatar World has free-text chat**, a play-time limit or ads today.
- The **exact counts of packs and items** in either game, and the cost to own everything in Avatar World.
- Whether the **hospital and mall are free in Toca now**, and whether Pazu PLUS includes Avatar World.
- Whether the unicorn theme, a dinosaur or an ocean theme is the stronger hook (open in `docs/concept-state.md`).

## 5. Decisions this suggests for you

- Little mode: **explore, care and dress, not build** (recommendation 5).
- Free cap on creatures: **none or generous** (recommendation 2).
- Multiplayer at launch: **no** (recommendation 8).
- A recorded playtest with 4-6-year-olds before locking the core loop (recommendation 12).
