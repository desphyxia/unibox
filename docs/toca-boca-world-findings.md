# Toca Boca World: Gameplay Findings (Video Analysis)

Status as of 2026-10-12. Pass 1 (frames only, sections 3-8) and Pass 2 (audio transcription, section 9) are both complete. Section 9 also lists corrections to Pass 1.

## 1. Purpose and scope
Identify the key gameplay elements of Toca Boca World from gameplay videos, so the most important aspects can inform the unicorn sanctuary game (see `docs/concept-state.md`). This covers Toca Boca World only; Avatar World is a later pass.

## 2. Method and limits
- **15 videos** chosen to cover: first-time play, walkthroughs, one long child play session, update showcases, shop and economy, a parent review, a monetization critique, and tutorials.
- **Frame-only pass:** each video was downloaded locally, sampled at a fixed interval (about 18-40 frames per video, 139 s for the 84-minute session), tiled into timestamped contact sheets and read. Timestamps below are from those sheets, so each is the sample point, not an exact event time.
- **What frames can't show:** audio (commentary, music, sound design), touch input when the video has no hand-cam, short interactions between samples, what an icon does unless a video shows its effect, and anything about progression, economy tuning or retention.
- **Bias:** these are creator videos, so they show what creators choose to show. Counts like "7 of 15" mean "visible in 7 of these videos", not how often players do it. 15 videos is a small, non-random sample.
- **Dates:** the videos span 2018-2026 and the game's UI changed over that time. One video (V9) is from the older "Toca Life World" branding and its prices are out of date.
- **Not committed:** the downloaded videos and screenshots stay out of the repo. This document is text only.

## 5. Detailed findings (Pass 1, frames only)

### 5.1 Navigation and HUD
- The HUD is consistent: an orange back arrow top-left (replaced by a home button on the world map), a sparkle button and a green shop cart next to it, a blue shapes button top-right (often with a red notification dot), a sun/chick time icon, and a people icon bottom-right (character tray). Older builds (V5) use different icons, and the sparkle button is absent in V2.
- The title screen is a small planet with a large Play button. The shop cart sits beside Play, with a restore-purchases icon, a grown-ups icon and settings top-right (V7, V13).
- Rooms are side-scrolling. Moving between locations uses a fade, an elevator with floor buttons, or a door, and almost always passes through a loading screen.
- The world map is an aerial view you pan and tap. Zones differ in season and theme (autumn hills, farmland, city).

### 5.2 Character creator and roster
- A mannequin-in-a-dome dressing room (V1, V3, V7, V14) with category tabs along the top, undo/redo and a confirm check. A second head-and-body creator has tabs for skin, hair, hair color (with slider), eyes, nose, brows, mouth, freckles/makeup, glasses, hats and clothes (V14).
- Roster: a bottom tray with a heart (favorites) and character slots, a "+" button to create more, and a saved-characters grid with a 15-slot cap shown in V7 ("12/15", "14/15"), delete and duplicate buttons.
- V4 (true first run): the first character is made before anything else, with a prompt that more can be made on later visits.
- V6 (a child's long session): the child returns to the creator at least four times over 84 minutes, including to make mermaid characters (new content).
- A promo strip inside the creator's tab bar reads "Create and customize even more!" with a SHOP button (V6, V14). Shop upsell sits inside the creative flow.

### 5.3 Locations and role-play
- Locations are small, dense and detailed: homes, a supermarket, a bakery, a police station, a farm, a forest camp, a school, a hospital, a marine lab, a fishing boat, food trucks, a concert stage, a fashion store. Each has several interactive props.
- Several characters are placed in one scene (up to 4-7 visible). Players use them as a family or group (V5, V8) and pose them.
- Character poses and animations appear to include sitting, carrying items (a tray of food, a juice box), emotes and idle behaviors (V8). The "movements update" (V8) adds animated reactions such as sleeping, hugging, sickness and shock, with a "Zz" shown above a sleeping character.
- Costumes and props as play: an umbrella, a creature costume, diving suits, instruments.

### 5.4 Interaction patterns
- Where a hand-cam is visible (V2, V3, V4), play is **taps and drags**: tap an object to trigger it, drag to move or place, swipe to pan. Players poke at many props rather than follow a path.
- Selecting a placed object shows a white or red outline, a move handle, a delete handle and a "swap" icon to cycle variants (V6, V15).
- Object states persist while you stay: food stays on the table, candles stay lit, items placed stay placed (V1, V15).

### 5.5 Home building
- **Build on the map:** tap a "+" spot, then place a house, with a move and delete handle on built houses and an "ALREADY BUILT" tooltip (V4, V6, V10, V15).
- **Decorate inside:** an empty room, a right-hand catalog with category icons (bed, bath, storage, seating, tech, decor, tools) and a collapse arrow. Furniture snaps into place and layers (rugs, wall decor, lamps).
- Notable furniture: a working fireplace with animated fire, hanging plants, string lights, kitchen sets and posters. V11's creator frames it as "15+ mins of decorating".
- A "PRINT IT" machine (V4, V10, V11) lets the player print a character picture, apparently as a poster. I only saw its UI, not the result.

### 5.6 Time of day, weather and seasons
- A sun/chick icon sits top-right, and the sky color changes through a session: dusk to night over about 25 minutes (V5), a night harbor (V6), sunset on the map (V7).
- V3 shows a **manual panel** with sun, moon, rain, snow and cloud icons. So time and weather are player-controlled in the game, not only automatic. It's the closest thing to your "reactive world" differentiator.
- Seasons appear as **separate map zones**, such as an autumn hillside (V1, V7, V13), rather than as a calendar that changes the world. A snowy street shows in V3.

### 5.6b What Toca doesn't show in this sample
Absence in 15 videos is not proof, but I saw no sign of: creature needs or moods, breeding or genetics, storytelling written for parents, any numerical progress, quests, fail states, or in-app learning scaffolding. The parent review (V13) frames learning as incidental (creativity, role-play).

### 5.7 Onboarding (V4, the only true first run)
Sequence with approximate times: create first character (0:00-0:47) → "Let's build a home" prompt → map: "Swipe to build your first home" (1:04) → loading → empty home with a delivered box (2:08) → furniture catalog opens on beds (2:40) → "Collect your first gift" (3:12) → a free starter location with a robot host (4:48) → the map with a shop banner (6:24) → free decorating. About 3 minutes to the first gift, with no account or sign-up screen visible and no tutorial text beyond short prompts.

### 5.8 Free gifts and content cadence
- A "Gifts every Friday!" TV in a post-office-style room (V3, V13) and "Free gift" bubbles on the title screen and map (V13).
- Free content drops are a recurring theme: "100+ items and a location" (V7), a free fashion store (V10, V11), and a "movements" update (V8).
- Official reply quoted in V10: the free fashion store ships with "45+ free items and furniture plus 15+ free clothing items" in the outfit designer. The same video says some items players saw were from a paid pack coming later, and that this confused viewers.

### 5.9 Shop structure
- **Three tiers** (V9, 2018; prices are in Canadian dollars, corrected in Pass 2): single-location packs at $1.39 (e.g., Bungalow, Pet Hotel, Barn, Daycare, Bistro), town bundles at $8.49-$10.99 (Biscuit Town $9.99 with 8 locations and 31 characters; Lettuce Lands $8.49; TipTop Tops $10.99) and a Mega Pack at $27.99 (25 locations, 127 characters). Larger single locations sit between: Business Tower $2.79, Hospital $3.99, Resort Hotel $3.99.
- Each card shows a hero image, price, location and character counts, a "What you get" strip, and a "Part of: $X pack" cross-link.
- Footer buttons: "How to add your apps" (import content from other apps by the same maker) and "Restore purchases".
- **2024 (V14, EUR):** style pack 0,99 €, furniture pack 1,99 €, Hospital 3,99 €, with bundles listed below and a "Feeling the 90's vibes?" themed row.
- A persistent shop cart button appears on nearly every gameplay screen. A star badge appears on the cart in 2025-2026 videos (V3, V13).

### 5.10 Purchase trust issues (claims from V12 and quoted comments, unverified)
V12 is a critical commentary video built from screenshots of comments and reviews. It claims:
- Players who paid for the hospital location were upset when it later became free (V12 at ~0:31, ~7:14; the hospital price in V9/V14 confirms it was paid earlier).
- Purchased bundles disappeared after reinstall or when switching accounts, and support did not respond (a quoted 1-star review, V12 at ~7:45).
- One quoted reviewer says their child asks for $30-$60 of add-ons and calls the model predatory (V12 at ~9:18).
- A quoted Reddit comment says many locations have recently been made free, which makes players doubt buying new ones (V12 at ~12:24).

These are one critic's selection of comments and reviews. They are not a measured rate. They line up with the criticisms already in your research (cost, lost purchases), and they bear on your one-time-purchase design.

## 6. Implications for the unicorn sanctuary game
These come from Pass 1; section 9.3 adds what the audio changes.

**Build in (strongest evidence):**
1. A **creature creator with a saved roster** (favorites, slots, duplicate), prominent and revisitable. It's the most-returned-to feature.
2. **Islands as themed, dense play spaces** with several tappable props each, and no goals.
3. A **map hub** (the archipelago) that doubles as navigation, the build surface and a gentle shop surface.
4. **Placing and decorating** with a catalog, snap, selection outline and a swap-variant control.
5. A **fast first run**: make a creature first, then get a gift within about 3 minutes, with no sign-up.
6. **Emotes without chat** (a small fixed emote set).
7. **Recurring free content rituals** such as a weekly gift.

**Where your differentiators have open space (Toca shows little here):**
- Needs and moods, breeding, a parent storybook, and learning built into systems.
- Time of day and weather are player-set in Toca (V3). Making them react on their own would be new. The location-vs-clock question in your revisit list matters here.

**Pitfalls the videos surface:**
- Don't mix free and paid items inside one location without clear labels (V10).
- Don't make paid content free later without a plan for existing purchasers (V12 claims).
- Make restore-purchases easy to find and reliable across devices (V9 footer, V7/V13 title-screen icon, V12 claims).
- A shop prompt inside the creator (V6, V14) may annoy; for a 4-10 audience with parental gates, keep upsells outside the creative flow.

## 9. Audio pass (Pass 2)

### 9.1 Method and limits
- Audio was transcribed locally with the Whisper "base" model (faster-whisper, CPU). Speech recognition errors are expected, and the game's name is often garbled (for example "Toka Boka", "Talker Balker"; in V14 the narrator's words were mis-heard as another game's name, but the frames and the shop sheet confirm it is Toca Boca World). I have normalized the name and paraphrased rather than quoted.
- **No commentary:** V1, V4 and V5 are music or game audio only, so the audio pass adds nothing for them. The transcripts for V1 and V5 came out empty or one word, and V4 produced no segments.
- **V6** (a child's long session) is multiple voices and noisy, so the transcript is low confidence. I read it for themes, not wording.
- Sound design and music can't be assessed from a transcript. Only the narrators' remarks about them are captured.
- Everything said is the narrators' claim. V12 in particular is an opinionated critique, and V13 reads like a scripted summary.

### 9.2 Findings

**Movement and animation (V8, early access to an update)**
- Characters now have idle "breathing" motion, walking animation when dragged, and the walk speed follows how fast the finger drags (slow drag, slow walk).
- Held items move with the character. Sitting poses differ by furniture (for example, knees face a different way on a couch).
- About two pages of emotes (~24), and two characters react at the same time: crying, in love, angry, sick, shocked and trembling, embarrassed, heartbroken, silly dancing, sneaky, cold with visible breath, kissing, nervous, confused and sleeping. The creator calls it one of the best updates ever. This corrects Pass 1, which only saw a bar of 12 emoji.

**Character creator (V7, V13, V14)**
- A new "ear" section adds earrings, hearing aids and earbuds, mixable per ear. The narrator reads it as inclusion, and says earrings were requested for years.
- New items sit at the bottom of each list and **sparkle until you've seen them**. Narrators use "sparkling means new and free" to find them (V7).
- 15 new character slots were added in that update, which matches the "12/15" and "14/15" counters seen in Pass 1.
- A random-character button next to the creator (V13, claim) and layering outfit pieces (V7). Items can be dragged onto characters (V7).
- Outfit designer items can be dragged into the world: placed on mannequins, hung in a store, or tried on in a fitting room, which changes the character's outfit (V10, V11).
- V14 says the shop content shown in the creator is all paid, and the free selection is large ("a lot to choose from").
- V14 compares the loading screens to a well-known life-sim's style, i.e., players read them as charming.

**Store and "secret" interactions (V10, V11)**
- The free fashion store can be built from a "New items available" button, then a plus on the map. It can be "closed up" and reopened with the shutters.
- A photo booth ("Print It"): pick a filter (black-and-white, toned, regular), a background, headshot or full body, and corner stickers; the photo prints as a poster you can place in frames. Both narrators call it a highlight.
- Small hidden interactions: tapping a square on the ATM cycles the money color (green, pink, gray/black); tapping display cases changes their color; a locker holds a wig and glasses you can put on a character. These are pure delight, not rewards.
- New music in this update is remixes of existing game music (V11).

**Home designer tips (V15)**
- There are three districts on the map for houses. A plus marks a free spot; delete a house by tapping X and swiping to the end; move a house onto a down-arrow spot; switch two houses by tapping one and then the other.
- Furniture can be flipped and layered. The creator teaches design heuristics: match wallpaper and flooring, use rugs to section a room, "framing" (symmetry around a central item), lights in threes, plants for color, a fireplace for warmth.
- Takeaway: the tool is expressive enough that players develop composition rules. Flip, layer, swap and snap are what make that possible.

**Content cadence and free drops (V3, V7, V10, V2)**
- Updates roll out unevenly: iOS first, Android later (V7), with players asking where the update is.
- Teasers build anticipation: a "mystery" content reveal on a set date and a "biggest house designer update ever" (V7).
- Free fashion gifts on three named Fridays in February 2026 (13th, 20th, 27th) (V10). Together with "gifts every Friday" (V3, V13), this confirms a **weekly Friday free-gift ritual**.
- A reviewer says the game gets frequent updates tied to seasons and holidays (V2).
- Locations show free vs. not-free states while browsing (V3 narrator remarks on a street being "not free" then "free").

**Audience and reception**
- Age statements differ by source: 4-8 (V13, claim), kids "as young as four or five" can play with drag and drop (V2), roughly 6-12 (V12, claim). Together with the 4+ App Store ratings from the earlier research, this supports a floor of 4 with a core at 4-8.
- Drag and drop is the one-gesture core, described as usable without help by 4-5-year-olds (V2).
- A limitation from V2: the open-ended design has no goals, and everything is available from the start, so some players get bored and only return for updates. V13 makes the same point more gently.
- The creativity, fine-motor, cause-and-effect (drag food into an oven and it bakes) and emotional-expression benefits in V13 are the narrator's claims, not measured.

**The child's long session (V6, low confidence)**
- The child acts as a director: voices several characters, assigns roles (officer, diver, shopper, chef), makes up a story (a fishing trip, a mermaid transformation, princesses, a shopping trip with a list, paying a cashier) and uses the creator mid-story as a **transformation tool** (becoming a mermaid, changing hair color). That is why the creator is the most-revisited screen.
- Frustrations visible in speech: a prop that can't be held, food items that aren't there, long waits. The child also notices time of day ("almost nighttime") and weather ("rainy season"), though the second is unclear.

**Monetization and trust (V12 and others, claims)**
- V12 (a critical commentator): the hospital location was made free in Feb 2026, and the studio did the same in November (movie studio) and for a school location, which upset players who had paid. It says the studio answered every refund complaint with the same copy-pasted "reach out to support" reply, and that parents report support never answers.
- It claims the brand began as pay-once with no ads or in-app purchases, was sold to a toy company in 2016, then moved to a free download with a shop. It also says the free part is thin (a few free areas and a partly free creator) and quotes players saying the free version is "lackluster".
- It repeats the purchase-loss story: bundles vanishing after switching devices, a restore button that doesn't reliably restore, and in one quoted case reset homes and removed gifts. It also calls out that players now wait for items to go free, and that a rival (Avatar World) is more generous.
- **My check:** the hospital as a paid item is corroborated by V9 (2018) and V14 (2024). The rest is the critic's account and quoted comments; I have not verified any of it.
- V9 explains how the shop works: Toca Life World was a hub for earlier standalone apps. If you owned those, they were added to the map automatically with a building animation. The packs mirror those old apps, and the narrator found it odd that the hospital is sold on its own instead of as an app.
- Official replies quoted in V10 say the free store includes 45+ items and furniture and 15+ clothing items, and that some items players saw were from a later paid pack. Both V10 and V11 narrators agree the update was split between free and paid, and V10's creator was upset that it was advertised as free.
- A contradiction to note: V13 says kids "explore for a one-time payment". V9, V12 and V14 show a free download with in-app packs, so V13 is loosely worded.

### 9.3 What the audio changes in the recommendations
- **Add to "build in":** a weekly free-gift ritual with a clear date; a "new and unseen" sparkle on newly added items; a short list of hidden tap-to-delight surprises; drag-speed-linked walking and paired emotes (animation polish is a selling point); and quick access to the creator from inside a scene, since children use it as a story device.
- **Strengthened:** the creature creator and roster (V6 shows it as the story's transformation tool), the photo/poster feature as a shareable-keepsake idea (relevant to your parent storybook), and inclusion options in the creator (V7).
- **Strengthened pitfall:** the "paid then free" and "lost purchases" stories are the most repeated criticism across V10-V12. Your one-time-purchase and restore-across-devices plan addresses it directly, but a "free later" policy must be decided up front and communicated.
- **Open gap that Toca itself admits:** with no goals and everything available, some players lose interest (V2, V13). Your needs-and-moods and seasonal layers might address that, which is a hypothesis to test.
